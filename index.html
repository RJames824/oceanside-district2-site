About Ricky

I lost. It was worth it.

I am a 38 year old father, husband, son, brother. I was injured racing dirt bikes when I was 16 years old becoming a paraplegic. I have had to overcome some adversities in my life, but it has made me who I am today. I might not have dealt with them as I should have, but I am very grateful where my life has taken me. I ran because I believed I could serve my city, and there was no other motive than being of service. I never relied on a campaign manager, treasurer, or anybody to show me the ropes. I worked very hard to figure out the system, and it was a great learning experience.

I'm not a career politician, and I never pretended to be the most qualified person in this city to sit on that dais. I take challenges head on and don't run from them because I can't run in the first place.

I identify as an Independent American because that's what I am. I didn't register Independent to hide anything. I'm not chasing both parties at the same time. I registered Independent because Oceanside City Council is a nonpartisan seat, and I would have voted like it is: not carrying water for a party, not chasing endorsements from either side, and not letting outside organizations or PACs tell me how to vote. I answer to District 2 residents. This was a nonpartisan race, and I ran it that way: for the residents and the residents alone, never for either political party.

I still want this city to be the best possible place for young families to live, and compassionate to all the seniors who love living here even with the rising costs. We are a diversified city and no one should be left out. We can find solutions to make the potential of this city a reality if we work together.

Here's the plain truth: I got my butt kicked. Rick Robinson, the Republican incumbent, and Emily Gonzales, the Democrat, both beat me, and not by a little. It was still one of the best experiences of my life. You learn the most when you're a little over your head and you fight to keep pace.

I don't have a college degree, and this was the best crash course in city politics I could have asked for. No lie, six months ago I didn't even know city council was a nonpartisan election. I barely knew what nonpartisan meant.

I encourage anyone to do it. It's only a winning proposition. Even though I lost, and lost by a lot, I've grown immensely and I'll be so much more prepared next time around. Thank you to everyone who gave me a shot. Let's keep making ourselves heard.

— Ricky James

What I Ran On

Some of what I ran on for a better Oceanside

Real problems deserve real solutions — not empty promises. Here's what I ran on, and what I'll keep pushing for from outside City Hall.

Independent — Nonpartisan

I never wanted to be the councilmember residents read about for the wrong reasons. No conflicts of interest, no backroom deals, no party loyalty tests — just steady, independent judgment on behalf of residents.

Fiscal Discipline — No New Taxes

The City's answer to years of budget strain was asking residents for a 0.5% sales tax increase. I thought that was backwards — it hits young families and seniors on fixed incomes hardest, and it lets the City avoid fixing how it spends before asking residents for more.

Reachable Councilmember — Your Voice at City Hall

Too many residents only hear from their councilmember at election time. I planned to hold real office hours, show up where residents already are, explain City decisions in plain language before they're made — not after, and make it easier for residents to actually show up and be heard.

Smart Development

I'm pro-growth — but growth that fits our roads, water, and services, not approvals rushed through because a developer asked first. The General Plan Update left South Morro Hills without its own Community Plan, and I'm still committed to getting one established — so residents there have an actual, adopted plan for how the area grows, not decisions made project by project.

Fix Roads & Sidewalks

Our roads and sidewalks are falling apart in parts of District 2, and basics like this should come before anything flashy. Potholes, cracked sidewalks, and drainage problems should get fixed first.

Clean Up Our Parks

Every neighborhood deserves safe, well-kept parks — not just the ones that get the attention. I wanted to make sure District 2's parks got their fair share of maintenance.

Support Fire & Police

Our first responders keep this city safe, and they need the staffing and equipment to keep doing it. I wanted Fire and Police to get what they need, not what's left over after everything else is funded.

Cancel Flock Cameras

The City has contracts for automated license-plate-reader cameras around Oceanside. Residents shouldn't have their movements tracked without a real conversation about it first. I wanted to end these contracts and protect residents' privacy.

Help the Unhoused

Homelessness in Oceanside needs a hands-on approach, not just another study or another check written to a program that isn't working. I wanted real, practical steps that actually get people off the street and into stability.

Protect Mobile Homes

Oceanside has some of North County's last affordable mobile home communities, and residents there deserve protection from being priced or pushed out. I wanted to keep these communities safe, stable, and affordable.

Improve Our Schools

Good schools are why families stay in a city. I wanted to partner with our schools — not just show up for a photo op — so families have a real reason to put down roots here.

Save Our Farmland

North Oceanside's farmland is part of what makes this city different from everywhere else in North County. I wanted to protect it instead of letting it get paved over acre by acre.

Promote Agritourism

Our farms shouldn't just survive — they should be a reason people visit Oceanside. I wanted to turn agritourism into a real draw for visitors and a source of local jobs.

Boost the Local Economy

I want to bring good businesses to Oceanside and back the ones already here instead of taking them for granted. A stronger local economy means more jobs and a stronger tax base without raising taxes on residents.

Protect and promote the assets that set us apart

No other North County coastal city has what we have. We should treat these as the competitive advantage they are — maintaining them, showcasing them, and building our identity and economy around them:

  • Oceanside Harbor
  • The Pier
  • Municipal Airport
  • Municipal Golf Course
  • Direct access to Camp Pendleton
  • First & last stop from Orange County
  • San Luis Rey River Trail
  • Playgrounds on the beach
  • Sprinter & Coaster transit stations
  • Frontwave Arena
  • Closest beach access to Temecula

My Pledge — A clean campaign

Municipal politics should run on honest relationships and reasonable compromise that protects residents. My vow was simple: I wouldn't solicit or accept money that came with an agenda. That started with developers seeking approvals and vendors chasing city contracts — but it didn't end there. I held the same hesitation toward activists, non-profit organizations, political parties, and PACs looking to advance an agenda through my seat. If I had taken a seat on the City Council, I'd have answered to residents, not to whoever wrote the biggest check.

So how did I fund a campaign? With a strong message, efficiency, genuine relationships, and transparency — not a war chest. I wanted to stay as neutral and open-minded as possible, so I could offer the most rational and unbiased judgment on every decision that came before me. I treated my campaign's finances the way I'd have treated the City's: carefully, efficiently, and strategically. Money alone won't always solve a problem — motive, discernment, and honesty are more effective.

A note from Ricky

Your time and your name mean more to me than your money

I wasn't asking for your money — I was asking for your support. I hoped to do what I set out to do on the strength of this community.

I wish giving could be anonymous, but the law requires it to be reported — just like my expenses. I wanted it that way. Transparency was the whole point.

Putting my money where my mouth is

I filed a Partial Salary Waiver with the City of Oceanside before the election

Pursuant to Article I.5, Section 2.1.70(d) of the Oceanside City Code and California Government Code Section 36516(f), I formally notified the City Treasurer that I would waive part of my Council salary if I won.

Oceanside Council compensation runs about $3,352 a month — $2,802.49 in base salary, plus roughly $350 for Community Development Commission meetings and about $200 for Harbor District meetings. I would have kept no more than $1,200; the remaining ~$2,152 every month would have gone, by law, to the General Fund for the City to use at its discretion.

How these numbers were tracked

We tracked the District 2 candidates — Rick Robinson, Emily Gonzales, and my own committee — along with the PACs active in the race, using the City of Oceanside's eFile system. The three committees didn't start on the same day (Robinson's opened 03/06/2025, Gonzales's 11/12/2025, mine in 2026), so these are full-campaign totals, not just this calendar year. Total Contributions is everything that came into a committee: donors, PACs, and committees, plus candidate loans, in-kind donations, and independent expenditures made in the candidate's support. Loans, in-kind donations, and independent expenditures aren't ordinary monetary contributions, so they're called out in green where they show up. Campaign filings continue after Election Day, so final numbers may differ slightly.

Figures come from every Form 460 and 24-hour report each committee filed, through each candidate's most recent filing on the City's eFile system. I didn't solicit contributions, and I pledged not to take money from developers, vendors, PACs, or any interest seeking to advance an agenda.
